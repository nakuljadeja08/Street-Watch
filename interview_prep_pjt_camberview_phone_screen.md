## 3. Know the firm

**PJT Partners (NYSE: PJT)**
- Advisory-focused investment bank founded by Paul Taubman, spun out of Blackstone in 2015.
- Four businesses: Strategic Advisory (M&A), Restructuring & Special Situations, PJT Camberview (shareholder advisory), and PJT Park Hill (private fund placement and secondaries).
- **Q2 2026 (reported July 27, 2026):** record revenue of $486M, up 20% YoY, and adjusted EPS of $1.97, up 28%. Both beat consensus. Growth came from strategic advisory, private capital solutions and restructuring. No funded debt, $535M cash. Management said full-year growth will be slower than the first half.

**PJT Camberview**
- Founded in 2012 by **Abe Friedman** as CamberView Partners. He was previously at BlackRock and Barclays Global Investors in governance roles and helped start Glass Lewis. PJT bought the firm in 2018 for about $165M. Friedman is a Partner and Head of PJT Camberview, based in San Francisco.
- **Its edge: "investor-led" advice.** The founders came from the buy side (index funds, active managers, pensions, proxy advisors), so they tell boards how investors will actually react.
- **Verticals:** shareholder engagement, **strategic investor relations** (this role), activism and contested situations, ESG and sustainability, and corporate governance.
- **Why it matters inside PJT:** Camberview gives PJT a relationship with boards *before* an M&A or activism event. Activist campaigns are increasingly built around M&A demands, so Camberview and the M&A bankers often end up working the same situations.

## 4. Industry context (good for "what's interesting to you right now")

From 2026 proxy season commentary (Cleary Gottlieb, Wachtell Lipton, Innisfree via Harvard Law Forum, Freshfields):
- **Activism is busy but rarely reaches a vote.** More campaigns were launched in H1 2026 than in H1 2025, but only about 6 U.S. fights went all the way to a vote, versus 9 in 2025. Settlements are the main way activists get board seats.
- **About 40% of campaigns had an M&A thesis.** Activists are targeting larger companies and tech.
- **Shareholder power is fragmenting.** Proxy advisors are losing influence and passive giants are splitting their voting (pass-through and client-directed voting). SEC guidance has changed how passive holders can engage, and Rule 14a-8 is uncertain.
- **What this means for IR:** companies can no longer "win" by persuading ISS, Glass Lewis and the big three index funds. They need a real, continuous dialogue with a broader and less predictable shareholder base. That is the pitch for strategic IR advisory, and a natural thing for you to say you find interesting.

**Why PJT Camberview?**
It's the leader in investor-led shareholder advisory. The founders came from the buy side, which matches how you think. You'd work on high-stakes situations (activism, governance, IR strategy) at a firm known for small teams where analysts get real responsibility.
