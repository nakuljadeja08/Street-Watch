Dear Hiring Team,

At Bernstein's client conference I was the most junior person on our team, yet by the second day several international investors were coming straight to me. It was not because I knew more than the senior analysts. It was because I could take a dense research view and give them the two or three points that mattered. What made that work was knowing exactly where the line sat: what we had published, what we had not, and what I could and could not say in the room.

That line is what draws me to compliance at a16z.

In sell side research, compliance is part of the daily work. I studied for and passed the SIE, Series 7, Series 63 and Series 87, and the Series 87 in particular made me learn the rules that govern how research is written, reviewed and shared. Covering 22 U.S. Food and Broadlines companies, I authored more than 10 published notes and contributed to more than 30 others, and I fielded time sensitive requests from institutional clients on live calls. I learned to slow down when it mattered, check before answering, and escalate when something was unclear. I also tend to fix repetitive work rather than repeat it: I used SQL to automate the buildout of our historical earnings datasets, which is the same instinct your team describes as operationalizing compliance tasks to achieve scale.

I also know the venture side from the inside. At Cathay Innovation in Paris I supported the €100 million Ledger Cathay Fund, joined founder calls with six Web3 startups and helped on a co investment memo. Today I work with Impact Nano on its Series C, managing outreach to more than 20 investors and supporting their due diligence. Both roles showed me how much sensitive information moves through a fund in a single week: founder data, deal terms, portfolio updates. At a firm that invests across AI, crypto, bio and fintech, and from seed to growth, keeping the restricted list and information barriers right is what lets the investing teams move fast safely.

I know this role asks for more years in regulatory compliance than I have, and I would not pretend otherwise. What I can bring is licensing that is current, research training under real compliance review, firsthand experience inside both a venture fund and a company raising from one, and the attention to detail of a physics graduate who learned to break messy problems into testable parts. I would work closely with the Deputy Chief Compliance Officer and learn the program quickly.

Thank you for your time and consideration. I would welcome the chance to talk.
